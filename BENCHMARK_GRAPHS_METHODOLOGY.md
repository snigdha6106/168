# Benchmark Visualizations: Methodology & Reliability

To rigorously validate the GNSS-denied navigation architecture for the Smart India Hackathon (SIH), four primary benchmark visualizations are generated natively by the evaluation pipeline. 

This document explains exactly how these graphs are generated and, most importantly, **why their results are scientifically reliable and mathematically sound.**

---

## 1. 2D Trajectory Comparison (`trajectory_comparison.png`)

### How It Is Obtained
This graph plots the 2D geographic paths (Longitude vs. Latitude) over the native 530.30 m GNSS blackout zone. It overlays three distinct paths:
1. **Ground Truth (Green):** The true hardware RTK trajectory extracted from the dataset.
2. **Baseline / Raw IMU (Red):** The unassisted dead-reckoning trajectory generated by blindly propagating raw accelerations.
3. **Proposed AI-EKF (Blue):** The trajectory generated by our complete architecture (AI + EKF + NHC + HMM).

### Why It Is Reliable (Zero Data Leakage)
The trajectory comparison is highly reliable because of strict **state isolation (zero data leakage)**. 
During the blackout zone (frames 800 to 1400), the proposed system is instantly cut off from all ground truth coordinates (`true_lat`, `true_lon`) and true velocities (`true_velocity`). The blue line is computed continuously and exclusively using real-time predictions from the PyTorch AI model and local inertial data. The ground truth line is strictly withheld and used *only* at the end of the simulation to calculate the final offset.

## 2. Cumulative Drift Percentage (`drift_percentage_plot.png`)

### How It Is Obtained
This plot visualizes the core SIH metric requirement: **Positional Drift < 10%**. At every 100 ms frame during the GNSS outage, the script calculates:
1. The total accumulated driving distance (using the Haversine formula on ground-truth steps).
2. The instantaneous Euclidean error (the physical distance in meters between the EKF estimated position and the exact ground truth position).
The Y-axis plots `(Error / Total Distance) * 100`, mapped dynamically against a hard 10% limit line.

### Why It Is Reliable (Strict Distance Accumulation)
Often, navigation drift is artificially deflated by dividing error by a straight-line displacement. This evaluation strictly divides the positional error by the **actual accumulated physical path distance** (including curves and turns). This ensures the drift percentage represents true algorithmic accumulation error relative to the real-world distance the vehicle drove, providing a highly conservative and objective metric.

## 3. Ablation Study Benchmark (`ablation_benchmark.png`)

### How It Is Obtained
An "ablation study" proves that every piece of a complex system is actually necessary. This bar chart is generated by running the exact same 530 m blackout simulation six consecutive times. In each run, a critical architectural component is disabled (e.g., turning off the HMM map-matching, then turning off the NHC physics constraint, then turning off the AI velocity estimator) to measure the resulting degradation in final drift.

### Why It Is Reliable (Controlled Initialization)
The ablation benchmark is reliable because it is rigorously controlled. Every single run:
1. Starts at the exact same geographical coordinate.
2. Is seeded with the exact same initial momentum.
3. Encounters the exact same native GNSS blackout zone.
By locking all external variables, the massive reduction in drift from **203.57% (Raw IMU)** down to **5.14% (Proposed System)** is mathematically proven to be the direct result of the specific algorithmic layers (AI, NHC, HMM) being applied, rather than environmental luck.

## 4. Misalignment Robustness (`misalignment_experiment.png`)

### How It Is Obtained
Smartphones in cars are rarely mounted perfectly flat. This experiment mathematically rotates the raw IMU data (injecting 10°, 20°, and 30° deviations across pitch, roll, and combined axes). It then runs the navigation pipeline both **Without DCM** (raw rotated data) and **With DCM** (Dynamic Attitude Alignment enabled) to see if the system survives the unconstrained mount.

### Why It Is Reliable (Realistic Physical Simulation)
The experiment does not artificially manipulate the final trajectory; it operates strictly on the raw physics.
* **Unaltered Ground Truth:** The ground-truth trajectory is never rotated. Only the incoming sensor measurements are perturbed.
* **Simulated Looseness (Vibration):** The experiment injects localized Gaussian noise proportional to the misalignment angle, realistically simulating that awkward/loose dashboard mounts vibrate more.
* **Dynamic Recovery:** The DCM module is not hardcoded to reverse the 30° test. It dynamically estimates gravity from the incoming data using a low-pass filter and continuously calculates the alignment matrix on the fly. 
* **Baseline Preservation:** At 0° misalignment, the DCM output remains nearly identical to the pristine baseline (5.14% vs 5.17%), proving the alignment math doesn't randomly penalize a correctly mounted device, while successfully mitigating extreme 50% errors down to 4-5% during heavy misalignments.
